Your Knock account starts with a single environment: production. [Enabling environments](#enable-environments) adds a development environment where you can build and test changes before you promote them to production. After that, you can [create additional environments](#create-additional-environments) (for example, a staging environment to mirror your own development lifecycle.) For an introduction to environments and when to use them, see the [version control overview](/version-control/overview).

## How environments work

Each Knock environment has its own isolated data (such as users, tenants, and objects) and its own version-controlled content (such as workflows, guides, and layouts.)

### Send data to environments

Each environment has a set of **data resources** that are per-environment and will never be shared between environments. These data resources are not version-controlled.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="See all per-environment data resources" anchorSlug="see-all-per-environment-data-resources">
    The following data resources are per-environment and not version-controlled:

    - Users
    - Audiences
    - Tenants
    - Objects
    - Messages
    - Analytics
    - Logs
    - Events
    - Broadcasts

  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

An example: the users you identify in Knock. Your real production users are identified in your production environment, while your test development users are identified in your development environment.

Each environment has its own set of API keys which you use to send data to the environment. You can find your environment-specific API keys under "Platform" > "API keys" in the Knock dashboard.

### Create content in environments

Each environment has a set of **content resources** that are managed via version control. These are resources associated with the creation and orchestration of the content you send to your customers.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="See all version-controlled content resources" anchorSlug="see-all-version-controlled-content-resources">
    The following content resources are version-controlled:

    - Workflows
    - Guides
    - Layouts
    - Partials
    - Translations
    - Reusable requests

  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

Here's how content resources work:

- Content can be created in any environment.
- Content can be promoted upwards through environments.
- Content can only be edited in the environment in which it was created.
- Content is versioned with [commits](/version-control/commits). You can roll back to earlier commits at any time.

These rules enable two ways of working with content and shipping it to your end users in production. Every account can create content directly in production. Once you've [enabled environments](#enable-environments), you can also create content in development and promote it.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Create directly in production" anchorSlug="create-directly-in-production">
    For content managed by growth, marketing, and product, such as feature announcements and lifecycle workflows, this path is faster and doesn't require the promotion step.

    1. Create and update the content in your production environment
    2. Publish (commit) the changes to your production environment when ready

  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Create in development and promote to production" anchorSlug="create-in-development-and-promote-to-production">
    For content managed by engineering, such as high-volume transactional workflows, this path ensures that content can only be updated in development before being promoted to production. It requires [enabling environments](#enable-environments).

    1. Create and update the content in your development environment
    2. Commit the changes to your development environment
    3. Promote the changes to your production environment when ready

  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Enable environments

Enabling environments adds a development environment below production, and turns on [promotion](/version-control/commits#promoting-commits), [additional environments](#create-additional-environments), and [branches](/version-control/branches). To decide whether your team needs them, see [this frequently asked question](/version-control/overview#faq-when-to-enable-environments).

<Steps titleSize="h4">
  {/* prettier-ignore */}
  <Step title="Go to the Environments page">
    Navigate to the <a href="https://dashboard.knock.app/~/settings/environments" target="_blank">**Environments**</a> page under the **Version control** section of your dashboard's account settings.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enable environments">
    Click **Enable environments**, then confirm.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Enabling environments requires the `owner` or `admin` role, or a custom role with environment management permissions. You can't turn environments off after you enable them.

### What changes when you enable environments

- **Your Development environment starts fresh.** Knock copies your `default` [email layout](/integrations/email/layouts) into development and nothing else. Your workflows, guides, and other content stay in production. This is expected: development is a new place to build, not a copy of production. You can [clone](#clone-resources-across-environments) individual resources into development from the dashboard, or use the [CLI](/cli/overview) or [Management API](/developer-tools/management-api) to pull production resources and push them to development in bulk.
- **Existing content stays editable only in production.** Content can only be edited in the environment where it was created, so resources you built in production stay there. To work on one in development and promote it, [clone it into development with the same key](#faq-move-resource-from-production-to-development).
- **Development becomes your default environment.** The [Knock CLI](/developer-tools/knock-cli) and [Management API](/developer-tools/management-api) target your account's default environment when you don't specify one. Before you enable environments, that's production. After, it's development, so pass `production` explicitly in any scripts that should keep targeting it.
- **Members gain access to development.** Account seats with the `member` role can work in any environment. To keep a team member limited to production, assign them the Production-only member role. Learn more in [roles and permissions](/manage-your-account/roles-and-permissions).
- **Environment permission settings appear.** The [Modify resources in any environment](#modify-resources-in-any-environment) and **Promote resources** settings appear on the <a href="https://dashboard.knock.app/~/settings/permissions" target="_blank">**Permissions**</a> page in your account settings.

### Modify resources in any environment

Once you've enabled environments, the **Modify resources in any environment** setting on the **Permissions** page controls whether members can create and edit content directly in environments other than development. This setting is enabled by default.

1. **Enabled.** Members can create and edit content in any environment, including production.
2. **Disabled.** Every environment other than development is read-only. Members create content in development and promote it to production.

The **Promote resources** setting on the same page controls who can promote changes between environments. When it's disabled, only account seats with the `owner` and `admin` roles can promote.

## Clone resources across environments

You can clone supported resources from one environment to another in the Knock dashboard. Cloning creates a new copy of the resource in the destination environment. The clone gets a new key by default (with a `-copy` suffix) and is independent from the original.

Open a resource's action menu and select **Clone** (or **Duplicate** for email layouts) to start a clone. When your account has more than one writable destination environment, you can choose which environment to clone into.

For most resources, cloning is a dashboard-only feature and is not available through the [Knock CLI](/cli/overview) or [Management API](/developer-tools/management-api). [Goals](/concepts/goals) are the exception: you can also clone them with the Management API or with [`knock goal clone`](/cli/goal/clone).

### Supported resources

You can clone the following resources across environments:

- Workflows
- Broadcasts
- Guides
- Message types
- Email layouts
- Audiences
- Goals
- Partials
- Translations
- Reusable requests
- Source event mappings

Archived resources cannot be cloned.

### Clone vs promote

Clone and promote solve different problems:

1. **Clone.** Creates a new resource in another environment. Use this when you want a copy to exist in both environments, or when you need to copy content to an environment lower in the promotion chain (for example, from production to development).
2. **Promote.** Moves committed changes for an existing resource up through your environment chain, keeping the same key. Use this when you are following the development-to-production workflow and want the same resource to exist across environments.

When you clone a workflow, Knock also copies its channel step templates and workflow schema into the destination environment.

### Environment targeting

Which environments you can clone into depends on the [modify resources in any environment](#modify-resources-in-any-environment) account setting, following the same rules as creating and editing content:

1. **Enabled.** You can clone into any environment in your account.
2. **Disabled.** You can only clone into your development environment.

Branch environments cannot be used as clone targets.

### Common use cases

1. **Copy a broadcast from development to production.** Create and test a broadcast in development, then clone it to production when you are ready to send it to real users.
2. **Copy production content back to development.** If you created a workflow directly in production and want to edit it using the promotion model, clone it into your development environment. You can give the clone the same key as the production resource if you plan to promote it back and overwrite the production version later.
3. **Duplicate a workflow for experimentation.** Clone a workflow within the same environment (or into another environment) to try changes without affecting the original.

## Create additional environments

Once you've [enabled environments](#enable-environments), your account has development and production environments. If you need an additional environment in Knock to mirror your own development lifecycle (for example, a staging environment), you can add it on the settings page of the Knock dashboard.

To create a new environment, go to the <a href="https://dashboard.knock.app/~/settings/environments" target="_blank">**Environments**</a> page under the **Version control** section of your account settings. You'll see a button to "Create environment."

### Choose where the new environment goes

Environments are ordered, and that order determines your promotion path. When you create an environment, the **Insert after** select in the create modal determines where it lands. Choose the environment that the new environment should come directly after, and Knock inserts it one "level" above that environment.

Two constraints apply to the position you choose:

- **You cannot insert an environment above production.** Production is always the highest environment, so it isn't available in the select.
- **You cannot insert an environment below development.** Development is always the lowest environment, so the new environment will always sit above it.

By default the select is set to the second-to-last environment, which places the new environment directly below production. When your account has only development and production, that means a new environment is inserted between the two.

Environments cannot be re-ordered after they're created, as this would break the promotion model for previously-promoted changes.

### What's in a new environment

A new environment is not empty. Knock seeds it from the environment directly below it when you create it, and different parts of your account are seeded in different ways:

| Resource                                                                                                                    | What the new environment starts with                                                                                                                                                                               |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Workflows, guides, layouts, partials, translations, and reusable requests                                                   | Promoted from the environment directly below it (the latest committed version of each item; archived items are skipped).                                                                                           |
| <a href="/concepts/variables" className="text-brand underline">Environment variable</a> values                              | Empty. Values are per-environment and you need to set them.                                                                                                                                                        |
| <a href="/concepts/channels" className="text-brand underline">Channel</a> settings                                          | Provider credentials are not copied and you need to configure them.                                                                                                                                                |
| <a href="/integrations/sources/overview" className="text-brand underline">Source</a> settings                               | Every source in your project appears in the new environment with default settings and its own newly generated source URL. No settings are copied, so you need to point your provider at the new environment's URL. |
| <a href="/preferences/hosted-preference-center" className="text-brand underline">Hosted preference center</a> configuration | The default configuration.                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| API keys                                                                                                                    | A new public and secret key pair.                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| Users, tenants, objects, messages, and logs                                                                                 | Nothing. Data is never copied between environments.                                                                                                                                                                |

## Rename an environment

You can rename any additional environment that you created. Your Development and Production environments cannot be renamed.

<Steps titleSize="h4">
  {/* prettier-ignore */}
  <Step title="Go to the Environments page">
    Navigate to the <a href="https://dashboard.knock.app/~/settings/environments" target="_blank">**Environments**</a> page under the **Version control** section of your dashboard's account settings.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select the environment you want to rename">
    Open the action menu next to the environment you want to rename, select
    **Edit environment**, update the **Name** field, and click **Update**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Update any slug references">
    Knock derives an environment's slug from its name, so renaming an
    environment also changes its slug. Update the following after you rename an
    environment:
    <ul>
      <li>
        **CLI commands and CI pipelines.** Any `--environment` flag that uses
        the old slug, such as `knock workflow list --environment=staging`.
      </li>
      <li>
        **Management API requests.** Any `environment` or `to_environment` query
        parameter that uses the old slug.
      </li>
    </ul>
  </Step>
</Steps>

A renamed environment keeps its unique ID, its API keys, and its position in your promotion path. Workflows, guides, layouts, and other content are untouched, and no data moves between environments. Any environment-specific [custom permissions](/manage-your-account/roles-and-permissions#custom-permission-groups) are also preserved on the renamed environment.

Renaming an environment requires the `owner` or `admin` role, or a custom role with environment management permissions.

<Callout
  type="info"
  title="Branch environments cannot be renamed."
  text={
    <>
      A <a href="/version-control/branches">branch</a> takes the name you give
      it when you create it, and that name is fixed for the life of the branch.
      If you need a different name, delete the branch and create a new one.
    </>
  }
/>

## Environment access controls

We recognize the importance of protecting your sensitive data, so we designed Knock from the ground-up with privacy and security in mind.

There are two tools you can use to control access to your data in the Knock dashboard:

- [Roles and permissions.](/manage-your-account/roles-and-permissions) Knock offers built-in roles for common team functions, and on Enterprise plans [custom permission groups](/manage-your-account/roles-and-permissions#custom-permission-groups) with per-environment access controls. For example, you can give support team members access to debug issues without permission to change notification logic, or deny access to an environment entirely.
- [Customer data obfuscation.](/manage-your-account/data-obfuscation) Use environment-level controls to hide customer data for every team member in an environment. On Enterprise plans, [custom permission groups](/manage-your-account/roles-and-permissions#customer-data-obfuscation) can also set per-environment obfuscation rules.
